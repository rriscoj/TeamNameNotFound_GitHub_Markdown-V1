Server team: share the job, then wait or work?

Many servers can share a job, but combining the answers and waiting for each other costs time. Check Activities 6.3 and 6.4, then find out what makes the waiting smaller.

What to notice
  • With the defaults: 1 worker needs 39 steps, 4 workers 12 steps (9 of own work + 3 to combine), 8 workers 11 steps and 40 workers 39 steps. These are your paper answers to Activity 6.3.
  • The red curve goes down, then up again: after about 5 to 8 workers, every extra worker adds more combining than it saves.
  • Switch to combining in pairs: the combining part shrinks a lot, but it never reaches zero. The cost never disappears.

Servers that must wait for each other

Each round a server works, then waits for a sync with the others. Share of time working = work time ÷ (work time + sync time). The shortest possible sync is a round trip at light speed in fiber (Chapter 5): 2 × distance ÷ 200,000 km/s. Inside one data center we assume 1 ms.

What to notice
  • With 100 ms of work per round and 1,000 servers, your paper answers to Activity 6.4 appear: 99%, 91%, 50% and 33% working, which is about 990, 909, 500 and 333 servers.
  • Try 10,000 km with 1,000 ms of work per round: the share goes back up to 91%. More work between syncs means less waiting.
  • Try 10,000 km with half of the waiting hidden: the share rises to 67%. Hiding all of it is the dream; real systems never manage that.
