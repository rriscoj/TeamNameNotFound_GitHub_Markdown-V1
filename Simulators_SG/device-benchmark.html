Device benchmark: which hardware does which AI need?

Seven devices, three AI jobs. First read the benchmark results (made-up numbers) and find each device's weakest link. Then use the numbers in a self-driving car: can it stop for a pedestrian?

Run the benchmark

Device A is the reference: 16 GB RAM, fast CPU, strong GPU, 5G. Devices C, D, E and F change one thing compared with A; devices B and G change several. Choose an AI job and a device.

What to notice
  • Chatbot, Device C: the 3G network changes nothing (0.2 s, as Device A), because the chatbot runs on the device. Device D (slow CPU) takes 4 s: 20 times slower.
  • Image generation, Device F: only the GPU is outdated, but it takes 45 s, 9 times slower than Device A. A slow CPU (Device D) costs only 3 times (15 s). The task decides which part matters most.
  • Self-driving car: every device except A is too slow or fails. Device C is fine only if it uses Edge AI: with the Cloud it waits 0.5 s.
  • This is a made-up benchmark of one chatbot, one image model and one car. Real benchmarks use standard tests and many runs, and real chatbots also use GPUs.

Can the car stop for the pedestrian?

A pedestrian steps out. The car moves speed × reaction time before the brakes start, then needs braking distance = speed² ÷ 20 (speed in metres per second). It is safe if the total is at most the distance to the pedestrian.

What to notice
  • With the start settings (54 km/h = 15 m/s, pedestrian 15 m away) the braking distance is 11.25 m. Device A reacts in 0.1 s, moves 1.5 m, and stops with 2.25 m to spare. Device C with the Cloud waits 0.5 s, moves 7.5 m and needs 18.75 m: a crash.
  • Tick the Edge AI box: Device C reacts in 0.1 s like Device A and is safe.
  • Slow the car to 30 km/h: the Cloud version of Device C now stops in time (7.6 m), but Device D (2 s) still needs 20.1 m and crashes. A slower car gives every device more room, but only up to a point.
  • The highest safe speed column shows how fast each device could drive for the distance you chose. Devices E and G never react, so their safe speed is 0.
  • The braking rule (speed² ÷ 20) is a simple model; real braking depends on the road, the tyres and the car.
