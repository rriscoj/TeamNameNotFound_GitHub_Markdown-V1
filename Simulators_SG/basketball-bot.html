Robot Basketball: train the robot and design its reward

A robot learns to shoot by trial and error: it tries a power level, gets points, and updates what it knows. First train it and watch exploration turn into exploitation. Then change the reward rules and see how a badly designed reward makes the robot cheat.

Let the robot practise

The robot stands 5 m from the hoop and chooses Power Level 1, 2 or 3. A swish gives +10, a miss gives −5. It starts knowing nothing. Each shot, with the chance you set it explores (a random level); otherwise it exploits (the level with the best average reward so far).

In the calm court Level 1 always falls short, Level 2 always swishes and Level 3 always hits the backboard. The robot's knowledge is the average reward it has seen for each level.

What to notice
  • With the start settings (20% exploration, 50 shots, calm court, seed 1) the robot finds Level 2 early and then uses it almost every time. Each random shot costs points once the best level is known.
  • Move exploration to 100%: the robot never uses what it learned, keeps shooting at random and scores much less. Move it to 0%: the robot only uses what it knows, and in the calm court that still works, because a bad level looks bad at once.
  • Switch to the windy day: results vary, so the robot needs more shots to be sure which level is best.
  • The simulator is a simple "try, score, average" learner. Real robots use more complicated methods, but the loop is the same.

What is the robot paid for?

The robot has two ways to earn points for one minute. Careful shot: it lines up a Level 2 shot, takes the time you set, and always swishes. Wild throw: it throws at once (1 second) and swishes only 5% of the time. Choose the rules, and the robot picks whatever earns more points per minute.

What to notice
  • Original rules (+10 swish, −5 miss, careful shot 10 s): careful shots earn 60 points a minute, wild throws lose 255. The robot plays basketball.
  • Broken rules (+1 for every shot, no penalty for a miss): wild throws earn 90 points a minute and careful shots only 66. The robot spams wild throws: it has found a loophole. This is reward hacking.
  • Put the miss penalty back (fixed rules): wild throws lose 195 points a minute, so careful shots win again.
  • With the broken rules, slide the careful-shot time down to 7 seconds or less. The loophole closes, because the careful robot can now earn more (at 8 seconds it still cheats). A reward can be safe or hackable depending on the numbers, so engineers test it.
  • The wild throw (1 second, 5% success) is a made-up model of a cheat. Real hacks are harder to predict, which is why designers watch what the robot actually does.
