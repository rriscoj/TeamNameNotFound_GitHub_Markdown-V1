Network lab: packets, distance and download time

Three small experiments on a simplified network: see what distance costs even on a perfect network, send a message in packets (with and without checking), and see where a faster plan stops helping.

What to notice
  • UDP is fast but anything lost stays lost. Packets can also arrive out of order (lines that cross).
  • TCP notices missing packets (after a waiting time) and sends them again, and it hands data to the app in order. That is reliable, but the message takes longer.
  • Set loss to 0% and jitter to 0: both behave the same. Raise the loss and compare the two columns.
  • Use UDP-style for a live video call or a game (fresh data matters more than old data). Use TCP-style for a file or a web page (every packet must arrive).

Why distance costs time, even on a perfect network

Light in a fiber cable travels about 200,000 km per second. Round-trip time = 2 × distance ÷ 200,000 (then × 1,000 for ms).

What to notice
  • Check your paper answers to Activity 5.3: 0.1 ms, 10 ms, 100 ms and 200 ms. Set the extra time to 20 ms for the challenge.
  • No better hardware can beat the speed of light. That is why servers close to the user (edge computing) help.

What to notice
  • For the 100 GB game: about 22 hours at 10 Mbps, 2.8 hours at 80 Mbps and 13 minutes at 1 Gbps (your paper answers to Activity 7.2).
  • For the 1 KB message the line is flat: about 50 ms on every plan. The wait is latency, not bandwidth.
  • Lower the latency to 5 ms and compare: only then does the tiny message get faster.
