Thermal throttling: how hot does the chip get?

A chip makes heat when it works, and the case gets rid of some of it. If the chip gets too hot it slows itself down. Pick a chip, a cooling method and the outside temperature, and watch the temperature and the speed.

1) Pick the chip, the cooling and the weather

If it never slowed down, the chip would settle at: outside temperature + power ÷ cooling.
The chip slows itself down when it reaches 85 °C. Then it works only as fast as the cooling can handle: speed = cooling × (85 − outside) ÷ power (at most 100%).

What to notice
  • With the start settings (mid-size chip, sealed case, 25 °C) the chip would settle at 125 °C, so it slows down after about 183 s and then keeps only 60% of its speed. In 10 minutes it does 72% of the work of a chip that never slows down.
  • Move the outside temperature to 40 °C (a robot in the noon sun): the speed falls to 45% and the slowing down starts after about 120 s.
  • The powerful GPU in a heat sink only keeps 40% of its speed, and in a sealed case only 15%. It needs the fan, and a small robot has no room for one.
  • The low-power chip never slows down in any of the three cases at 25 °C. That is why robots and watches use such chips.
  • A real chip is more complicated (it has several temperature sensors and many speed levels). This model shows the idea, not a real device.
