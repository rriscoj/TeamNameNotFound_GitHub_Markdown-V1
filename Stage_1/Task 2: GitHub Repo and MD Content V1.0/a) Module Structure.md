# Develop Module Structure: Hardware, Network Infrastructure, and Behaviourist AI

## 1) Intended Learning Outcome
The outcomes aim to develop both foundational knowledge and practical problem-solving skills that learners can build upon in further study. Learners should be able to:
- Understand the fundamental concepts of computer hardware, data networks, and how artificial intelligence learns through trial and error (behaviourist AI).
- Explain in simple terms how hardware components (like processors, memory, and sensors) and network connections affect how fast smart devices and AI applications can run.
- Grasp the basic ideas behind reinforcement learning, including how an AI agent interacts with its environment using actions, states, and rewards.
- Through hands-on activities and simple simulations, explore how real-world gadgets (like delivery robots, smart home devices, and self-driving vehicles) make decisions while dealing with battery, memory, network latency, and speed limits.

## 3) Prerequisite Knowledge Learners Need
Learners are expected to have everyday experience using computers or smartphones, basic middle-school arithmetic skills (understanding simple scores, percentages, and cause-and-effect rules), and a general curiosity about how technology works. No prior coding, advanced math, or electronics experience is required. 

## 4) Planned Examples, Demonstrations, Questions, or Activities
The study guide uses relatable everyday analogies, such as comparing a computer's memory to a study desk, a network to a traffic highway, and AI rewards to earning points in a video game or training a pet with treats. It also features visual grid-world activities where learners can adjust point rewards to see an AI change its behavior, paired with beginner-friendly review questions and short explanations after each chapter.

### Content Application
**Application in Study Guide**
Key definitions and core concepts are explained step-by-step. Practical mini-projects and visual walkthroughs help high school students see how concepts apply in real life. Summaries and practice quizzes make reviewing simple and effective.

**Application in PPT Slides**
- Slide decks keep text minimal by using clear diagrams, comparison tables, flowcharts, and bullet points so students do not get overwhelmed.
- Diagrams show step-by-step processes (e.g., how data moves from memory to the processor).
- Simple tables contrast basic ideas (e.g., standard processors vs. AI chips).
- Bullet points highlight main ideas clearly without clutter.

**Application in Video Tutorial**
Video lessons focus on visual learning with animated flowcharts, screen demonstrations of simple simulations, and clear voiceovers that break down abstract concepts step-by-step.
